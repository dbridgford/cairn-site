Getting started

Cairn keeps your notes, scanned paperwork, tasks and ideas in one place, on your own devices.

Home is where you capture: tap Note, Scan, Dictate or Task. Below that are things due soon, pinned notes and what you worked on last.

Notes lists everything, newest first. Pull down to sync, swipe left to move a note to the Trash, swipe right to mark a task done.

Tasks groups notes the GTD way: Inbox (just captured), Next (do these), Waiting (someone else), Scheduled (comes back on its date) and Someday.

Notebooks holds your notebooks, tags, ideas, projects, smart views and the Trash.

Search looks inside every note and the text of scans, photos and PDFs.

Capturing notes, scans and photos

New note: tap Note on Home, or the pencil in any list. A note made inside a notebook, tag or project goes there.

Scan paper: tap Scan. Hold the iPhone over the page; Cairn finds the edges and straightens it. Add more pages, then Save. The scan becomes a PDF in a new note, and its text is read so you can search it.

Photos and files: in a note, tap the paperclip › Photos or Files.

From other apps: in Mail, Files, Photos or Safari, tap Share › Cairn (or Open in Cairn). The file becomes a new Inbox note.

Siri and Shortcuts: say “New note in Cairn”, “Scan with Cairn”, “Dictate in Cairn” or “New task in Cairn”. The same actions are in the Shortcuts app and can go on the Action button or Lock Screen.

Formatting: the bar under the note has bold, italic, headings, lists, checkboxes and tables.

Paste from other apps: Markdown (from ChatGPT, GitHub or a text file) becomes formatted text.

Dictating

Tap Dictate on Home to start a new note by voice, or the Dictate button inside a note to add to it. Words appear as you speak; tap Insert when you’re done.

Where your iPhone supports it, speech is turned into text on the iPhone itself and never leaves it. You can also use the microphone key on the keyboard.

Notebooks, tags, tasks and projects

Notebooks are like folders; each note is in at most one. Tags cut across notebooks: a note can have many.

Change a note’s status, notebook, due date and tags from the row under its title.

Give a task a due date by setting its status to Scheduled. On the day it moves to Next and Cairn sends a reminder at 9am.

Projects are outcomes that need several steps. Their actions are the notes in them; every active project should have a next action.

Ideas move from Spark to Exploring, Developing and Done. Tap Idea on Home to capture one. In an idea, the Stage, Category and Score chips under the title move it along, colour it and score it (impact, confidence and ease, 1 to 10); Score also holds its appetite, when to stop, a review date and its sub-ideas. Home › Ideas lists them by stage: swipe right to move one to the next stage. The Mac adds the ideas board, roadmap and releases.

Syncing with iCloud

Cairn syncs through your own iCloud account. Your notes go from your device to Apple’s iCloud and back to your other devices. They never pass through any server of ours.

Turn it on: Settings › iCloud Sync. Do the same on your Mac (Cairn › Settings › Sync) and on any other iPhone or iPad signed in to the same Apple Account.

What syncs: notes, notebooks, tags, tasks, projects, ideas, and attachments (scans, photos, PDFs). Search indexes and invoice data are rebuilt on each device.

Attachments download only when you open them, so your iPhone doesn’t fill up with your whole archive. The Mac chooses which files go to iCloud in Cairn › Settings › Sync › Attachments to upload: the last 12 months (the default), All, or none. A file older than that can’t be opened on iPhone until it’s set to All.

Edits on two devices: the most recent edit to a note wins. Earlier versions stay in Version History.

Storage: synced data counts towards your iCloud storage. If iCloud is full, Cairn tells you and keeps working; changes sync once space is free.

Sharing securely

Send a note or a whole notebook so only the person you choose can read it.

A note: open it, tap ⋯ › Share Securely. A notebook: touch and hold it in Notebooks (on the Mac, right-click it in the sidebar) › Share Notebook Securely.

Choose a passphrase of at least 12 characters, or tap Suggest a Strong Passphrase. Then send the file by email, Messages or AirDrop, and send the passphrase another way, for example read it out or text it, never in the same email.

Cairn file (for people who use Cairn): the notes, tags and attachments, encrypted with AES-256. Without the passphrase the file is unreadable. The recipient taps the file and chooses Cairn (on the Mac, double-click it or use File › Open Shared File…), enters the passphrase, and the notes arrive in a new notebook called “Shared: …”.

Protected PDF (for anyone): a PDF of the note that asks for the passphrase in any PDF reader.

Shared copies are separate: later edits aren’t sent automatically. Share again to send an update.

Encryption and security

On iPhone and iPad your whole library is encrypted by the device, with a key tied to your passcode. Scans and other attachments use the strictest protection: they can’t be read at all while the device is locked. Use a passcode, and Face ID or Touch ID.

On the Mac your library is protected by FileVault, which encrypts the whole disk. Cairn › Settings › Security shows whether it’s on and opens the setting if it isn’t. The library folder is readable only by your Mac account.

In iCloud your notes are stored in your private iCloud database, encrypted by Apple. Turn on Advanced Data Protection (Settings › your name › iCloud) for end-to-end encryption.

Locked notes are encrypted again with their own password everywhere: on each device, in iCloud and in backups. Lock a whole notebook from its menu.

Safety: files that arrive from other people (shared files, sync) are checked before they’re stored, and on the Mac, programs and scripts in attachments are shown in Finder rather than opened, so macOS can check them first.

Locked notes

Lock a note that holds bank details, passwords or contracts: open it, tap ⋯ › Lock Note.

The note’s text is encrypted (AES-256) with your locked-notes password. The same password works on all your devices. It isn’t stored anywhere, so if you forget it the text can’t be recovered. Keep it in your password manager.

On iPhone you can unlock with Face ID: Cairn keeps the password in the iPhone’s Keychain, readable only after Face ID.

Titles stay visible so you can find locked notes, and attachments aren’t encrypted.

Sharing, printing and PDFs

In a note, tap ⋯ › Share as PDF. The PDF has the note first, then its PDF attachments, which is handy for sending an accountant a bundle of invoices.

⋯ › Print prints the note. Copy Link to Note gives a cairn:// link; paste it into another note to connect them. Notes that link to a note are listed at its foot as Linked from.

Version history and the Trash

Cairn keeps an earlier version of each note for every 10 minutes of editing (the last 50). ⋯ › Version History shows them; tap one, then Restore. Your current text is kept as a version too.

Deleted notes go to the Trash (Notebooks › Trash). Swipe to restore. Empty Trash deletes them permanently.

Privacy and your data

Cairn has no account, no tracking and no analytics. Your library lives on your device; with sync on, a copy lives in your private iCloud database, which only your Apple Account can read.

Text recognition and (where supported) dictation run on the device. Nothing is sent to us. The full privacy policy is in Settings › Privacy Policy.

Troubleshooting sync

“Sign in to iCloud”: open the Settings app › your name, and sign in. Make sure iCloud Drive is on.

Nothing arrives from the Mac: check sync is on in both apps and both are signed in to the same Apple Account. A large library can take a while the first time; the status in Settings shows progress.

“iCloud storage is full”: free up space in iCloud or buy more storage. Cairn keeps your changes and sends them later.

Still stuck: Settings › Sync Now, or turn sync off and on again (your notes on this device are kept). Then email support from Settings › Contact Support.
